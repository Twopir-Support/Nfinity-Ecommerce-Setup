E-commerce Management Setup

Why a direct-to-consumer brand needs one place to see every order.

Picture a direct-to-consumer cheer footwear brand. Shoppers buy on Shopify and on Amazon. A 3PL, ShipMonk, ships the Shopify orders; Amazon ships its own FBA orders; ShipStation prints labels and tracks parcels; Accounting Seed keeps the books. Every order touches four or five systems, and each one holds only part of the story.

We built the E-commerce Management Setup so that Salesforce becomes the one place where the team sees each order, its stock, its shipments, its invoice and any sync that failed. The four screens in this preview follow that same scenario.

The problem, and what changes

WhoBeforeWith the hub
Customer supportChecks Shopify, ShipMonk, ShipStation and Amazon to answer one “where is my order?” question.Opens one Order 360 page: status, every shipment with tracking, and a timeline of what each system did.
OperationsCannot see ShipMonk and FBA stock side by side, so an order can sell stock that is not at the right warehouse.Sees stock by location. Each order reserves stock where it will ship from, and low stock is flagged.
E-commerce leadHears about failed or duplicate syncs only when a customer complains.Sees failed syncs first, with a Retry button. A repeated order message is blocked by its External Order ID.
FinanceMatches shipped orders to invoices by hand in a spreadsheet.Sees on each order how much is shipped and how much is invoiced, with each Accounting Seed invoice listed.

How an order moves through the hub

  1. Order arrivesShopify or Amazon order becomes an Order with lines. Its External Order ID is unique, so a repeat is blocked.See Operations home
  2. Stock is reservedShopify orders reserve at ShipMonk, Amazon orders at FBA. Available stock drops at that location.See Product stock
  3. 3PL confirmsShipMonk confirms the lines. The order path moves to Confirmed.See Order 360
  4. Parcels shipEach parcel is a Shipment with carrier and tracking. An order can ship in parts.See Order 360
  5. Invoice postsAn Accounting Seed invoice (simulated) is recorded against the shipped units.See Customer
  6. Every step is loggedEach system message writes a Sync Log row. Failures show on the home page and the order, ready to retry.See Sync logs

The data model behind it

Nine objects. The five standard ones carry customers, products and orders; four small custom ones carry what standard Salesforce has no place for.

Standard objectCustom objectOrder, the hub

The same model as a tree: what belongs to what.

  • AccountThe shopper
    • ContactName, email, phone
    • OrderOne per Shopify or Amazon order
      • OrderItemOne per line; points to a Product2
      • Shipment__cOne per parcel
      • Invoice__cSimulated Accounting Seed invoice
      • Sync_Log__cOne per system message
  • Product2One per SKU
    • Inventory_Balance__cOne per location
      • ShipMonk WarehouseShips Shopify orders
      • Amazon FBAShips Amazon orders
    • OrderItemEvery order line that sells this SKU
ObjectTypeWhy it exists
Account, ContactStandardThe shopper. Amazon buyers are masked, so Amazon orders share one account.
Product2StandardOne record per SKU: style, colour and size.
Order, OrderItemStandardThe order from either channel, with ordered, confirmed, shipped and invoiced quantities per line.
Inventory_Balance__cCustomStock for one SKU at one location (ShipMonk or FBA): on hand, reserved, inbound, available.
Shipment__cCustomOne parcel: carrier, tracking, status. Lets an order ship in parts.
Invoice__cCustomThe simulated Accounting Seed invoice, so shipped and invoiced can be compared.
Sync_Log__cCustomEvery message between systems: success, failure or duplicate. Drives the timeline and the error view.

Following one order

  1. Oct 15, 9:12 AMMadison orders two Apex shoes and a backpack on Shopify. Order SHP-10234 appears in Salesforce and three units are reserved at ShipMonk.
  2. Oct 15, 11:06 AMShopify sends the same order again. The hub recognises the External Order ID and blocks it: no second order, no second reservation.
  3. Oct 15, 4:10 PMThe backpack ships by USPS and its invoice posts. The order is now partly shipped and partly invoiced.
  4. Oct 16, 2:20 PMOne shoe ships by UPS. The second cannot ship: ShipMonk has none available, 48 inbound. The line turns amber and the product page shows why.
  5. Oct 17, 8:02 AMA ShipStation tracking update fails. It shows as an alert on the order and on the home page, with a Retry button.
  6. Oct 17, morningMadison asks where her shoes are. Support opens one page and answers in seconds: one parcel delivered, one in transit, one unit waiting on stock.

Where this setup fits

Any brand that sells on Shopify and Amazon, ships through a 3PL and Amazon FBA, and keeps its books in Accounting Seed can use the same nine objects and the same flow. Before anything is connected live, discovery confirms which system owns products, stock and accounting.
